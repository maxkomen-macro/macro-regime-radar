The regime tag says Overheating at 26% confidence, but the real story is that Overheating and Stagflation are running neck-and-neck at 46/41. Today's tape reinforces both sides of that split: GM's beat and raised guide validate the demand-side heat, while $82 crude and gas back above $4 keep the inflation impulse alive. Nothing here argues for the Recession bucket at 6% — the consumer is not rolling over. The 10Y up 11bp on the month with 2s10s steepening 7bp is the bond market sniffing exactly this: growth is fine, inflation isn't done.

The interesting tension is that corporate pricing power (GM raising guidance) and energy-led input inflation are pushing the same direction on nominal growth but opposite directions on margins for everyone downstream of oil. If auto OEMs can pass through, who eats the crude move? That's the stagflation seed hiding inside an overheating print.

What's priced: a Fed that cuts into resilient growth because disinflation is assumed to continue. What's at risk: a Warsh-tilted Fed that stops cutting — or hikes the terminal expectation — if energy bleeds into core.

Watch next core PCE and the 5y5y breakeven for the inflation re-acceleration tell, and any Warsh or Powell commentary on the neutral rate. If WTI holds $80+ through the week, front-end yields go higher.
